Proposed title: A wayfinding eval for topic labels: identification is not fit (+ measuring the
detail_levels dial)

---

This continues the eval work from #173, but it ends somewhere more constructive than the last two installments: a working instrument, a finding about what label quality even is that replicates across two corpora, and a concrete suggestion about `detail_levels`.

The starting observation is that both instruments I had been using measure *fit*: is this label a true, well-formed description of this cluster. But Toponymy's own framing is cartographic, and a map label's deployment function is different. A reader looking at the map has to find the right region among its neighbours given only the name. That's a discrimination task with a verifiable answer, so it can be scored with accuracy rather than preference, which is exactly what the fit instruments lack (a grounded judge ties on about half of good-vs-good label pairs).

The instrument design comes from a side project of mine ([Atlantic Mirror](https://stevenfazzio.github.io/atlantic-mirror/), which pairs European and North American cities by character and captions each pair); the only honest way I found to score those captions was a police lineup, where a caption is good only if a reader can pick the right city out of a group of look-alikes given just the caption. A topic label has the same job. So the wayfinding lineup, as I've been calling it, works the same way: for each (cluster, label), a listener LLM sees the label plus five candidate document groups, the true cluster and its four nearest same-layer neighbours, each represented by five held-out member documents sampled to exclude the exemplars the namer saw (so parroting exemplar phrasing can't win). The listener scores every candidate 0-100, and the label's score is the probability mass landing on the true cluster. Distractors and documents are frozen per cluster, so different labels for the same cluster face identical lineups and every comparison is paired. Before trusting it I ran the floors: shuffled labels score at or below chance (0.04-0.12 vs 0.20), easy lineups with distant distractors hit ceiling (0.81-0.94), and repeated runs give a noise band that any claimed difference has to clear. All of it replicates on 20 Newsgroups and an arXiv ML corpus.

What it shows, in decreasing order of confidence:

- Real labels identify their regions well (prob-mass 0.55-0.57 against 0.20 chance), and known-bad labels order exactly as they should. The failure modes are legible rather than just low scores: give a cluster its sibling's label and the mass physically moves to the sibling's candidate (0.51-0.59 on the sibling vs 0.16-0.21 on the true cluster); give it "Various topics" and the mass spreads nearly uniform.
- Fit and identifiability are different axes. The degradation a grounded fit judge punishes hardest, on-topic padding, is the one the lineup barely notices (the weakest paired separation on both corpora, 79% and 83%). Padding hurts fit and reading, but it does not make a label harder to find. Overall the lineup correlates with the judge at rho 0.65-0.74: related, not redundant. On genuinely-good label pairs the lineup ties on most, like the judge does, but the pairs it does decide look right: it prefers the label with the distinguishing specific ("Larson's Reciprocal System Theory" over "Debates on Unified Theories in Physics" for the crank-physics cluster), and a small blinded check (me, rating pairs without knowing which the lineup picked) agreed with its picks 10 of 12 times when I could express a preference at all.
- Most of a compound label is identification-inert. Splitting the "X, Y, and Z" labels into conjuncts and re-running the frozen lineups (20 Newsgroups only for this leg): 87% of conjuncts can be dropped at no measurable cost, 96% of compound labels contain a single conjunct that identifies as well as the whole label at 40% of the length, and 12% of conjuncts actively hurt, because they pull mass toward a neighbour that owns that content.
- As a side effect, the eval is a pipeline integrity check. The easy-lineup floor is what caught the misaligned example data in #176: clustering ran fine, naming produced plausible-sounding vague labels, and the fit judge didn't complain, because a vague label fits a scrambled document sample about as well as anything. The lineup flagged it in one run, since identification is impossible when documents don't belong to their clusters.

Which leads to the actionable part. Toponymy currently sets label specificity open-loop: `detail_levels` maps each layer to a `SUMMARY_KINDS` word-count phrase (the finest layer asks for an 8-15 word name, the coarsest for 1-2 words), and while the templates ask for names "sufficiently detailed to be distinguished from other topics", nothing checks. The lineup is the checker. So I named every cluster at all seven rungs of the ladder (stock Toponymy machinery, same fitted clusterer), ran every name through the cluster's frozen lineup, and selected the shortest name whose identification is within the noise band of the best rung.

Evaluated on a held-out configuration as a Goodhart guard (fresh document draws, a different listener model), plus the grounded judge for fit: the selected names match the stock names on identification (p ~ 1) and on judge-rated fit, at 55% shorter on 20 Newsgroups and 64% shorter on arXiv. And the dial turns out to be wrong in both directions. The controller mostly shortens fine-layer names, but at the arXiv coarse layer the stock rung ("simple (1 or 2 word)") is the worst rung on the whole ladder, costing 0.18 prob-mass against a 4-word name. That matches intuition once you see it: 8-15 words is far more than a fine cluster needs to be found among its neighbours, and 1-2 words can be less than a broad region needs.

I read that as two separable suggestions. The free one: the fine-layer `SUMMARY_KINDS` defaults could be much terser at, per these measurements, no cost to either fit or identification; the curve of identification vs length is nearly flat, so most of those words buy nothing. The principled one: the right specificity per cluster is a measurable quantity, and "the shortest name that still identifies the region among its neighbours" is a defensible definition of it. (This is an old idea in the referring-expression-generation corner of NLG: the best description of a thing is the minimal one that picks it out from the distractors in context, "the red cup" only if there are other cups. Or, if you like the information-theoretic frame, the label is a lossy code for the region and the lineup measures whether it decodes.) To be clear about cost, the full controller is not cheap: seven naming calls plus about twenty listener calls per cluster (3,751 input tokens each), roughly 25 to 30 times the LLM traffic of stock naming, so I'm not proposing it as a default. Its natural uses are as a calibration tool (run once on a representative corpus to pick better static defaults; the tables in the write-up are exactly that run for these two corpora), for coarse layers only (few clusters, most-visible labels, and the layer where the dial is provably worst), or for maps that are fit once and read many times. And since shorter names should collide more, I checked the interaction with the existing disambiguation pass: during naming, the tersest rung does make the pass work slightly harder (5 renaming calls covering 13 of 74 fine-layer topics, vs 2 calls covering 5 at the most verbose rung), and on the final name sets the pass's own trigger finds nothing left to rename in either arm on either corpus (no name pairs within its similarity cap, no exact duplicates, no lineup-mates sharing a name). So the shorter names don't buy their savings back in disambiguation calls.

Caveats, covered properly in the write-up: two corpora, one namer (Haiku, which pads more than a terser model would); one listener family with a measured but nonzero noise band; and "no measurable loss" means at the resolution of 3-sample self-consistency, not proven equality. Full write-up with the tables and floors: [WAYFINDING.md](https://github.com/stevenfazzio/toponymy/blob/experiment/wayfinding-lineup/experiments/label_quality/WAYFINDING.md), code alongside it. The harness is small and general (any labeling of any clustering with documents attached), and I'm happy to adapt any part of it if it would be useful.
